Why do 95% of software products suffer from the "30-Day Retention Cliff"?

They focus on acquiring users, but neglect the psychological loop required to turn intent into a reflex.

In *Hooked*, Nir Eyal laid out the definitive 4-step framework for building habit-forming products:
1. Trigger (External & Internal)
2. Action (Fogg Behavior Model: B=MAT)
3. Variable Reward (Tribe, Hunt, Self)
4. Investment (Compounding Stored Value)

Until now, applying this framework required manual audits and qualitative debates.

Today, I’m excited to open-source **HookEngine**: a zero-dependency behavioral intelligence platform and AI agent skill for software teams.

🔍 **What HookEngine does:**
• Scans codebases and PRDs for friction (form fields, auth walls, cognitive load)
• Calculates your product's Habit Zone coordinate ($Utility \times Frequency$) and EAST score
• Simulates 30-day cohort retention across 5 user psychographics
• Conducts synthetic "5 Whys" root-cause interviews with churned users
• Generates production-ready code and copy remediation diffs (e.g. Endowed Progress onboarding)

Built purely with Python's standard library (zero external dependencies) and packaged as an open Agent Skill for Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and Antigravity.
