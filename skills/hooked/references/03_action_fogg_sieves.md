# Reference 03: Action — The Frictionless Execution

> *"To initiate action, doing must be easier than thinking."* — Nir Eyal

---

## 1. The Fogg Behavior Model ($B = MAT$)

Behavior ($B$) occurs when **Motivation** ($M$), **Ability** ($A$), and a **Trigger** ($T$) converge simultaneously:

$$\text{Behavior} = \text{Motivation} \times \text{Ability} \times \text{Trigger}$$

```
High Motivation ^
                |       * [Action Occurs]
                |      /
                |     /   ACTIVATION THRESHOLD CURVE
                |    /
                |   /     x [Action Fails: Trigger Sparks but Friction Blocks]
Low Motivation  +--/------------------------------------------->
               Hard to Do (Low Ability)          Easy to Do (High Ability)
```

**Strategic Maxim**: Increasing motivation is difficult and expensive. Elevating **Ability** (cutting friction to near zero) is the highest-leverage lever in product engineering.

---

## 2. The 6 Elements of Simplicity

Dr. Fogg outlines six dimensions of friction. A product fails if it taxes any of these excessively:

1. **Time**: How many seconds/minutes are required? (e.g. 1-click checkout vs. 5-page checkout).
2. **Money**: Does it demand credit card credentials upfront?
3. **Physical Effort**: How many clicks, taps, form fields, and keystrokes are required?
4. **Brain Cycles (Mental Effort)**: Does the user have to read paragraphs, understand jargon, or configure settings?
5. **Social Deviance**: Does the action make the user feel socially awkward or violate social norms?
6. **Non-Routine**: Does it clash with entrenched existing habits?

---

## 3. Cognitive Heuristics that Boost Action

* **The Scarcity Effect**: Limited availability or time-bounded opportunities increase perceived desirability.
* **The Framing Effect**: The contextual presentation alters value perception (e.g. "Save \$10/mo" vs. "Lose \$120/yr").
* **The Anchoring Heuristic**: People rely heavily on the first piece of information offered.
* **The Endowed Progress Effect**: Progress toward a goal increases motivation (e.g. a loyalty card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-punched gets completed significantly faster than a blank card with 8 stamps).
