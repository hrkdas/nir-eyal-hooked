# Reference 06: Ethics — The Manipulation Matrix

> *"Building habit-forming products is a form of mind control. With this superpower comes profound moral responsibility."* — Nir Eyal

---

## 1. Habit vs. Addiction

* **Habit**: An automatic behavior that yields neutral or positive utility to the user's flourishing (e.g. Duolingo, Strava, budgeting).
* **Addiction**: A compulsive, self-destructive dependency that materially harms the user's health, finances, sleep, or relationships (e.g. gambling, compulsive video gaming, social media doom-scrolling).

---

## 2. The Manipulation Matrix (The 2x2 Quadrants)

To determine the ethical positioning of a product, answer two fundamental questions:
1. *"Would I use the product myself?"* (Skin in the game)
2. *"Does the product materially improve the user's life?"* (Human flourishing)

```
                  Does it materially improve the user's life?
                                  YES                     NO
                         +---------------------+---------------------+
                     YES |   THE FACILITATOR   |   THE ENTERTAINER   |
Would I use the          | (Highest Integrity) | (Ephemeral Delight) |
product myself?          +---------------------+---------------------+
                      NO |     THE PEDDLER     |     THE DEALER      |
                         |  (Self-Deception)   | (Exploitation/Harm) |
                         +---------------------+---------------------+
```

### Quadrant Breakdown:
1. **The Facilitator (Highest Integrity & Success Rate)**: The creator uses their own product and it materially elevates human capability. Creators have deep empathy for user pain points because they are users themselves.
2. **The Peddler**: The creator claims the product helps others, but does not use it themselves. Prone to hubris and misaligned incentives.
3. **The Entertainer**: Games, novelty apps, and entertainment. The creator plays it, but it provides ephemeral distraction rather than life improvement. High churn; requires constantly shipping new titles.
4. **The Dealer (Predatory & Destructive)**: The creator hooks users on mechanics they would never use themselves, extracting cash or attention while inflicting material harm. The dealer is building an addiction trap.

---

## 3. Dark Patterns & Sludge Audit Checklist

* **Roach Motel**: Is it easy to subscribe, but requires emailing customer support or calling a phone number to cancel?
* **Confirmshaming**: Does the opt-out button say "No thanks, I hate saving money"?
* **Deceptive Timers**: Does the product show a "Flash Sale Ends in 04:59" countdown that restarts on page refresh?
* **Sludge**: Does the product introduce deliberate cognitive friction to prevent users from asserting their autonomy?
